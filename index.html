    How well has this worked?

    I replayed these rules over 80,665 past matches (15 leagues, 2012 to now, odds from football-data.co.uk). The honest results:

    • The chances are accurate. Once the bookmaker's margin is removed the new way, predicted chances matched real results to within about 1 point in every band.
    • Betting blindly loses. Backing every outcome lost about 2 to 4% of the money staked at the best price among several bookmakers, and about 6 to 7% at a single bookmaker's price.
    • VALUE bets looked profitable only when you could take the best price across several bookmakers. On 1,400 to 4,300 past bets they returned about +5% to +8% at that best price, and the result was statistically above zero. At a single bookmaker's price the return was between about -4% and +1%, so roughly nothing.
    • Be careful with that. The best price is often a stale or short-lived price. In real life it may have gone by the time you get there, your stake may be limited, and bookmakers restrict accounts that keep taking such prices. The past figures assume you could always get it.
    • Beating the final price pays. Bets that beat the closing market price by over 6% returned about +11% to +12%. Bets that were over 5% worse than it lost about 13% to 15%. That is why checking the price before you bet matters.
    • "Check price" bets are suspect. Bets with an edge over 12% won about 20% of the time against 26% predicted, and lost money. It usually means a stale or wrong price.
    • When to bet. Of the value prices the rules would flag, 63% were worse by kick-off and only 39% were still good value at the start. So a good price should be taken as soon as it has been checked.
    • Form and head-to-head add nothing. Across 79,000 matches, teams with better recent form or a dominant head-to-head record won no more often than the odds already implied. The bookmakers price it in, so this app does not use them.
    • Even now it is only an estimate. With over a thousand flagged bets the margin of error was still about 7 points. Recent seasons have far fewer qualifying bets, so the newest evidence is thin.

    So treat betting as paid entertainment, not income. If you only use one or two bookmakers, expect roughly to lose a few percent of what you stake over time, and only stake what you can afford to lose.

    New to betting? Read this first

    Odds of 2.00 mean a £1 bet returns £2 if it wins: your £1 back plus £1 profit. Odds of 1.50 return £1.50 for a £1 bet.

    Win chance is the market's view of how likely a result is, after the bookmaker's built-in margin is removed.

    Price vs market (called "edge" by bettors) shows how a bookmaker's price compares with the market average. +3% means about 3p expected profit for every £1 staked. Most prices have no edge at all.

    Average result is an average over many bets, not a promise for one. A bet with a positive figure can still lose. A bet with a negative figure can still win.

    Stake is what you put on. Suggested stakes use a quarter of the standard "Kelly" sizing, capped at 5% of your current budget. Where there is no edge, the app suggests a small flat 1% stake instead.

    Current budget is your starting budget plus or minus the bets you have settled, so stakes shrink after losses and grow after wins.

    In-play (live) bets are not included on purpose. Live prices change every few seconds and this data refreshes far too slowly, bookmakers build bigger margins into live prices, and fast bets mean you lose money faster too. Betting before kick-off, when prices are steadier, is the safer way to use this page.

    Rules to keep: set a budget you can afford to lose, never chase losses, and treat winnings as a bonus. This app does not predict results.

    How it works. Each bookmaker's margin is removed (taking proportionally more off long shots, which tested better on 30,000 past matches), then the chances are averaged to get a win chance. Edge compares the best price you can get with that average, with exchange commission taken off. It measures price against the market. It does not tell you who will win, and the market average can be wrong. A big edge is often a stale price, so confirm it on the bookmaker's site before betting. Horse racing is not covered by this data source. 18+ only. Bet only what you can afford to lose. BeGambleAware.org
